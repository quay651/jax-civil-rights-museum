The story

History happened here.

Jacksonville's place in the civil rights movement is too often left out of the national telling. These are a few of the chapters the museum will hold.

1866

1866

A college is founded for freedmen

Edward Waters College opens in the year after emancipation, becoming Florida's first independent institution of higher learning for African Americans.

1900

Five hundred children sing a new song

At the Stanton School, students perform "Lift Every Voice and Sing," written by principal James Weldon Johnson and his brother J. Rosamond Johnson. It travels the country and becomes the Black National Anthem.

1935

A beach of their own

A. L. Lewis and the Afro-American Life Insurance Company establish American Beach on Amelia Island, a place where Black families could enjoy the Atlantic in the era of segregation.

1953

Hank Aaron integrates the league

Playing for the Jacksonville Braves at Durkee Field, a young Hank Aaron is among the first players to integrate the South Atlantic League.

1960

Ax Handle Saturday

NAACP Youth Council members holding peaceful sit-ins at downtown lunch counters are attacked by a mob armed with ax handles near Hemming Park. Their courage becomes a turning point for the city.

A home for every chapter

The museum will gather these stories, restore the artifacts that prove them, and keep them in front of the next generation.

Inside the museum

Built to be walked through, not just read.

Designed for students on field trips, families, and travelers following the Civil Rights Trail across the South.

Restored artifacts

Objects, documents, and photographs from Jacksonville's movement, recovered and preserved so visitors can stand in front of the evidence.

Oral histories

Recorded accounts from the people who were there, kept for the generations who will never get to meet them.

Classrooms and programs

Field trips, workshops, and teaching resources that carry this history into Duval County schools and beyond.
